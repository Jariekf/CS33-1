7. Evaluation
============================================================

Goal:

The goal is to predict the sales price for each house.

For each Id in the test set, participants must predict the value of the SalePrice variable.


Metric:

Submissions are evaluated using Root-Mean-Squared-Error, also known as RMSE.

Metric URL:
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root-mean-square_deviation

The RMSE is calculated between:
- the logarithm of the predicted sale price
- the logarithm of the observed sale price

Taking logs means that errors in predicting expensive houses and cheap houses affect the result more equally.

Question:
What is a Getting Started competition?

Answer:
Getting Started competitions were created by Kaggle data scientists for people who have little to no machine learning background.

They are a good place to begin if you are new to data science or have just finished a MOOC and want to get involved in Kaggle.

Getting Started competitions are non-competitive ways to:
- get familiar with Kaggle's platform
- learn basic machine learning concepts
- meet people in the community

They have:
- no cash prize
- a rolling timeline


Question:
What is the difference between a private and public leaderboard?

Answer:
The Kaggle leaderboard has a public component and a private component.

This design helps prevent participants from overfitting to the leaderboard.

If a model is overfit to a dataset, it is not generalizable outside of the dataset it was trained on.

This means the model may have low accuracy on another sample of data taken from a similar dataset.


Public Leaderboard:

For all participants, the same 50% of predictions from the test set are assigned to the public leaderboard.

The score shown on the public leaderboard reflects the model's accuracy on this part of the test set.


Private Leaderboard:

The other 50% of predictions from the test set are assigned to the private leaderboard.

The private leaderboard is not visible to participants until the competition has concluded.

At the end of a competition, Kaggle reveals the private leaderboard so participants can see their score on the other 50% of the test data.

Scores on the private leaderboard are used to determine competition winners.

For Getting Started competitions, because they run on a rolling timeline, the private leaderboard is never revealed.
